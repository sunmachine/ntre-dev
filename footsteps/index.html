Technical proposal · Draft for review

Footstep Consistency Audit

Neotokyo Rebuild · branch footsteps/sound-consistency · 17 October 2026

Abstract

Footstep sounds in Neotokyo Rebuild were inconsistent: crouch walking made noise, looking around while crouch walking made noise, a stray step played when dropping into a crouch, crouched steps played louder than running, and other players heard different steps than the stepping player. This paper traces each case to its cause across ten instrumented play passes, reviews how the current rule came about, and proposes one footstep formula that every client evaluates the same way, without changing movement. Sections 1 and 2 state the problem and the proposal; sections 3 to 5 add the per-change breakdown, the supporting data and the references.

1Problem

Footsteps sounded inconsistent compared with other games in the genre. What a player heard from their own feet, what the server sent, and what other players heard often disagreed. Loudness was inconsistent too: some quieter ways of moving played louder steps than running.

Expected
  • Crouching is silent unless sprinting.
  • Breaking a speed threshold for any reason still makes noise: a slide from a running jump, being pushed.
  • Standing in place never makes noise.
  • Volume follows how you move: sprinting is the loudest, and a crouch sprint is quieter than a standing sprint but louder than a run.
  • Walking, aiming and crouching follow the same silent rule.
  • Movement feel stays the same.
What happened instead
  • Crouch walking was audible.
  • One last step played after pressing crouch.
  • Looking around or switching keys while crouch walking made footsteps.
  • Crouched steps played louder than a standing run.
  • Sprinting as assault, or as recon with most weapons, was as quiet as running.
  • Players out of sight but in earshot heard nothing from the server.
  • Steps while turning in place: reported, not reproduced.

1.1Why players heard different things

Each footstep is decided separately in three places. Each one runs UpdateStepSound, then IsMakingFootstepSounds decides whether the step is silent. The marks show where each defect sits.

Server

every player, every tick
  1. PlayerMove runs the step timer
  2. Silent movement threshold crouch last step
  3. Volume picked by speed against 80 or 220 u/s volume
  4. PlayStepSound sends to players who can hear the stepper but cannot see them origin

Your client

your own steps, predicted
  1. Same movement code, first prediction only
  2. Silent movement threshold crouch last step
  3. Volume picked by speed against 80 or 220 u/s volume
  4. Plays locally, surface from movement code

Other clients

your steps, as others hear them
  1. C_BasePlayer::Simulate estimates speed from position updates
  2. Silent movement threshold crouch last step
  3. Volume picked by speed against 80 or 220 u/s volume, then plays locally

1.2How loud each step was

Every audible step plays the surface's quiet or loud volume, picked by comparing speed with a fixed run speed: 80 u/s crouched, 220 u/s standing. Crouched steps are then scaled to 65%. Those two numbers come from the Source SDK, which crouches at a third of run speed. NEO's speeds sit on the wrong side of both.

1.3How the current rule came about

The first row is engine behavior inherited from the Source SDK. Each later step was a reasonable answer to the problem in front of the team at the time. Together they explain why master's rule depends on the aim and walk keys, and why some questions were deliberately left open.

WhenDecisionGoalLeft open
2019-05
SDK import, 4508d865		 Inherit the SDK step volume rule. A step is quiet below a run speed of 80 u/s crouched or 220 u/s standing and loud above it, and crouched steps are scaled to 65% (fvol *= 0.65). Stock engine behavior. The SDK crouches at a third of run speed (about 63 u/s), so its crouched steps stayed under 80 and were the quietest steps. Never retuned. NEO crouches at 0.75 of run speed, so every crouch walk passed 80 and played at 0.325, louder than a standing run at 0.2.
2024-06
#275, #363		 Follow the original Neotokyo: footsteps decided by speed alone, above 90 u/s standing and 60 u/s crouched. Parity with the original game, after a report that crouching still made footsteps. Nothing at the time.
2025-02
#877		 Implement those limits: standing above 90, crouched above 60. Parity. Every crouch walk speed (102 to 150 u/s) is above 60, so crouch walking made footsteps, as in the original.
2025-07
#1097, from #992		 Let the walk key decide: holding +walk is silent below a raised limit, so bunny hopping still makes noise. Walking speed was raised to at least 90. Players could not tell whether they were moving quietly. A held key gives a predictable answer, without a new HUD element. The code comment already named turning and changing direction as ways to gain speed. Raising walk speed changed movement.
2025-10
#1431		 Make aiming silent too, set the limit per class (0.7 × run speed, or 0.7 × crouch speed while crouched), and return walk speeds to parity. Aiming moved slower than walking yet made footsteps, and the walk speed change had altered movement. Parity against key-based sneaking was deferred to a later discussion. 90° turns made footsteps, and a higher limit was suggested. Slow analog movement without a key stayed audible, and a speed-only check was suggested.
2026-09
#2151		 Move the check into IsMakingFootstepSounds and let water splashes follow footsteps. Sneaking players can enter and leave water silently. No change to the rule itself.
Where this proposal sits
  • The standing rule from #1431 gives way to the crouched rule, so walking, aiming and crouching share one threshold.
  • Step volume follows the movement mode instead of the SDK's speed cutoffs. The SDK's 65% for crouched steps stays.
  • Crouching joins the quiet modes, with a threshold relative to the player's own crouch walk speed. Quiet is still opted into with a key, so the speed-only and analog input suggestions from #1431 remain open for standing movement.
  • The turn headroom answers the 90° turn issue raised in #1431, for crouching and walking.
The open parity question

Under the original game's 60 u/s crouched limit, a crouch walk makes footsteps. Under this proposal it is silent. #1431 deferred the parity discussion; this proposal takes a position for crouching, and that choice is worth discussing on its own.

#1097 also links a Discord thread and a change document, which are not summarised here.

2Proposal

Make every footstep follow one formula, decided the same way by the server, the stepping player and everyone listening. Movement speed and feel do not change; only whether and when a step sound plays.

1Quiet is opt-in. Crouching, walking and aiming are silent at their normal speed, for every class and weapon. Moving faster than that still makes noise: sprinting, sliding, being pushed. Outside those three, every step at 50 u/s or faster is heard, so a runner passing through walk speed stays audible.

2Pressing crouch, walk or aim holds back your next footstep. If you settle into that mode, it is dropped. If you let go too early, it plays right away. Jumping or grabbing a ladder forgets it, and landing from a jump always makes a step.

3Looking around while crouch walking or walking does not count as speeding up. Turning widens the threshold, and one brief switch of movement keys is forgiven.

4Footstep volume follows how you move. Sprinting is the loudest, running keeps its volume, and a crouch sprint is 65% of a sprint: louder than a run, quieter than a standing sprint.

2.1The footstep formula

The same shared code decides a footstep in three places: the server, the stepping player's own client, and every other client that can see them. The terms come in three groups: speeds, turning and timers. Each group gets one picture, and the formulas reuse the same letters.

sound threshold = GetNormSpeed * weapon scale * SILENT_MOVE_THRESHOLD
actual speed    = GetNormSpeed * weapon scale * stance modifier

Both sides carry the weapon, so the gun cancels out: a quiet mode is silent whenever its stance modifier is at or below SILENT_MOVE_THRESHOLD, 0.825. Crouch and walk are 0.75 and aim is 0.6, so all three qualify with every gun; sprinting multiplies by 1.35 or 1.6 and never does. On master the threshold was GetNormSpeed * 0.7 with no weapon term, so the headroom changed with the gun.

2.3What players will notice

  • Crouch walking is silent for every class and weapon, with or without aim or walk.
  • Crouch sprinting, sliding from a run, and being pushed while crouched still make footsteps.
  • No extra step when dropping into a crouch from a run.
  • Looking around while crouch walking stays silent; repeated wiggle walking does not.
  • Walking and aiming follow the crouched rule: the first wiggle step is forgiven, and walking with the BALC is silent.
  • Crouch sprinting is louder than running but quieter than a sprint, and sprinting is the loudest step for every class and weapon.
  • Players out of sight but within hearing range hear footsteps again.

3Breakdown of the proposal

Each change in the order a footstep meets it: first the formula's two parts, then the fixes that make other players hear the same answer.

3.1Crouched silent movement threshold

Fixed

Formula part 1, crouched line. Crouching used to be silent only while also aiming or holding +walk, so a plain crouch walk was audible. The threshold is now the player's own crouch walk speed plus 10%, including weapon slowdown. That is the same limit as 0.825 of run speed in the formula.

Before 
// neo_player_shared.cpp, IsMakingFootstepSounds
if (GetFlags() & FL_DUCKING)
{
    if ((IsInAim() || IsWalking()) &&
        speed <= GetCrouchSpeed() * 0.7f)
        return false;
}
After 
if ((GetFlags() & FL_DUCKING) && !isOnLadder)
{
    float crouchSilentGrace = 1.1f;
    // plus turn headroom, see Turning below
    // ground speed: other clients see vertical
    // speed when a player steps up stairs
    if (groundspeed <=
        GetCrouchSpeed_WithActiveWepEncumberment()
        * crouchSilentGrace)
        return false;
}
Weapon slowdown

Every weapon slows its holder, by 0.675× (PZ) to 1.0× (BALC). The threshold first used the class crouch speed alone, so a recon crouch sprinting with a 0.725× rifle (147 u/s) stayed under 165 and made no sound. The threshold now uses the crouch speed with the active weapon applied, which keeps the same 10% margin for every weapon.

3.2Held step when starting a crouch, walk or aim

On branch

Formula part 2. Side-stepping at normal speed and pressing crouch often plays one more footstep. The game only counts a player as crouched once the 0.4 s crouch animation finishes, and a running step fires every 300 ms, so one step almost always lands in between. Walking settles in about 50 ms and is rarely affected, but follows the same rule so both keys behave alike.

Why release within one step plays it

Without that rule, a player could repeat crouch, settle, release about every 0.7 s. They would move at full speed for most of each cycle, since the crouch animation keeps run speed, and drop one step per cycle. Giving the step back on an early release means a dropped step always costs a real step's worth of crouched movement. Tapping crouch can only delay a step, never remove it.

Movement is unchanged

The rule only decides whether and when a step sound plays. Speeds, acceleration, the crouch animation and hull sizes stay exactly as they are. Only one step is ever held, and anything still loud after settling plays as before.

Other players get the same answer

Each of the three footstep deciders runs this rule itself, so each needs to know when crouch, walk or aim is held. Walk and aim already are: m_fIsWalking and m_bInAim are sent to every client in NEO (hl2mp_player.cpp:112, neo_player.cpp). For crouch, the engine already defines FL_ANIMDUCKING, "in the process of crouching" (public/const.h:150), inside the m_fFlags every client receives. Nothing in this codebase set it before. The branch sets it while crouch is held (CGameMovement::Duck), which needs no new network data.

Limits

Other clients see press and release about one snapshot late, so a penalty step can sound a few tens of milliseconds later for them than for the player. A second step before settling plays the earlier held one and holds the new one, so a crouch that never settles cannot skip a step either.

3.3Turning and key switching while crouched

On branch

Holding a movement key while turning the view, or switching keys such as W+D to W+A, really raises ground speed: acceleration builds toward the new direction faster than friction removes the old one. The gain reaches about 50% of crouch walk speed, more than the 10% headroom, so looking around while crouch walking made footsteps. The turning chart in section 2.1 shows the threshold against the simulated gain.

Turn headroom 
float crouchSilentGrace = 1.1f;
// crouch sprinting stays audible while turning
if (!m_fIsSprinting)
    crouchSilentGrace += 0.45f *
        Min(m_flFootstepTurnRate / 180.f, 1.f);

The turn rate comes from the view yaw, which every client receives, keeps its peak and fades over 0.25 s.

One-step forgiveness

Switching keys does not turn the view, so turn headroom cannot see it. The first crouched step over the threshold is skipped when the player has been crouched on the ground for 0.5 s, is not sprinting and is no faster than 1.55 c. It comes back after 1 s under the threshold.

3.5Step volume by movement mode

On branch

The SDK picks a quiet or loud step volume by comparing speed with a fixed run speed, 80 u/s crouched and 220 u/s standing, then scales crouched steps to 65%. Those numbers fit the SDK's own crouch, a third of run speed (about 63 u/s), so its crouched steps stayed under 80 and quiet. NEO crouches at 0.75 of run speed, so every crouch walk passes 80 and gets the loud volume. Standing, an assault sprint stays under 220 with every weapon and a recon sprint only passes it with a 0.85 weapon, so most sprints get the quiet one.

3.6Landing step

On branch

A flat jump falls too slowly for the engine's landing sound, which needs 350 u/s of fall. The footstep players hear on landing is the regular step whose timer ran out in the air, firing on the first tick back on the ground. Two things had broken that. Since #2151 UpdateStepSound restarted the step timer even in the air, so the first step after landing came at a random phase up to half a second later. And with crouching a quiet mode, a crouched landing at walk speed made no sound at all.

Problem

Crouch-jumps and drops landed silently, and standing landings had a step somewhere in the next half second rather than on impact.

Fix

The step timer waits at zero while off the ground, as in the SDK, so after a jump the step lands on the landing tick. A landing after at least 0.1 s in the air marks the next step decision as never silent, which also covers short drops whose timer has not run out.

A standing jump in place still makes nothing, since speed is under 50 u/s on landing, as before. The engine's hard-landing sound is unchanged.

3.7Server footstep origin

Fixed · PR #2230
Problem

In NEO builds, the only line that set feet sat inside #ifndef NEO, after #2151 removed the unused locals around it. The server then chose recipients from an uninitialized position.

Fix

Move VectorCopy( vecOrigin, feet ); out of the #ifndef NEO block. Upstream PR #2230 makes exactly this change. The branch carries a local copy until it merges.

Behavior change: players who are out of sight but within hearing range get the server's footsteps again, as in the stock SDK.

4.4Footsteps while turning in place
