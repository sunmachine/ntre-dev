Gun Handling: Gate 4 Component Review

Firing patterns and recoil are only one part of the puzzle when working on weapon feel. This review will showcase what additional details we can compose with our weapon handling to really make our gunplay feel great. Please follow this procedure and give me the review feedback on Discord @sunmachine:

  1. Play each clip. The numbered rows under it jump to a part, and 0.25x and 0.5x slow it down.
  2. Pick a verdict under the clip and add a note where something looks off. Each card carries a clip number, and the summary uses the same numbers.
  3. At the bottom, copy the summary and send it to @sunmachine on Discord.

Review expectations

  • Already measured

    1. Nothing changes by default

    No review needed. With none of the new components switched on, every gun handles as it does today. An automated comparison of 1219 handling values across 23 guns found no differences. An automated firing test of all 23 guns raised two prediction errors, both on the ZR68C. We think this may be a found bug, or it may be caused by spectator quirks in the dev listenserver during testing.

  • Needs review

    2. Each component can be seen working

    Each new component was switched on for one gun, the MX. The automated checks passed: the game accepted every setting, and switching the components on added no prediction errors between client and server. Whether each component can be seen, and whether it looks right, is what the clips below are for.

  • Needs review

    3. The camera setting at 0 removes every camera effect

    A player can switch camera feedback off with one setting. Measured: the roll peaked at 0.564 degrees with the setting at 1 and at 0.00018 degrees with it at 0, and the eye did not move at 0. The side by side pair in clip 5 is the check by eye.

Climb arrival, return and the punch spring

Look for. In part 1 the view should climb, pause, then come back about half way. Parts 2 and 3 have no climb: the view should bounce more loosely and settle later than stock.

Read first. Part 1 plays the climb's arrival and its return about 4.5 times slower than in play. Those two run on the real clock, and the capture wrote about 270 frames in each real second for a 60 frames a second clip. The end angle is exact: half the climb came back (pitch -1.0 to -2.7 against -4.4 without the return). Judge the timing in a live game, not here.
