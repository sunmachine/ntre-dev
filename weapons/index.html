Gun Handling Audit

Neotokyo Rebuild · branch weapons/feel-investigation · Revision 9 · updated 19 October 2026, 00:56 UTC · all revisions

How every gun fires, what the numbers say about balance, and one synthetic score per gun for how useful it is. Players say the MX is very strong. The code agrees, and the main reason is a damage breakpoint, not a spread or recoil value.

1The question

Each gun's handling comes from three places: its weapon script (game/neo/scripts/weapon_*.txt), one shared handling table in weapon_neobasecombatweapon.cpp, and a few per-gun overrides in the weapon headers. Nobody has laid these side by side. This page does that, runs a seeded simulation of every gun against every class, and turns the result into one comparable number.

Three things to answer:

  • How does each gun fire? Where do the bullets go, shot by shot.
  • How is it balanced? What each gun trades for what.
  • How useful is it? A usefulness quotient (UQ) that can be compared across guns.

2Findings

  1. The MX kills an Assault in 3 high-tier hits; the rifles around it need 4 or 5. A global damage modifier of 1.485 (sv_neo_wep_dmg_modifier) turns the MX's 28 damage into 41.6 per hit. Three hits is 124.7, just over the Assault's 120 HP. The only other primaries with a 3-hit Assault kill are the semi-auto M41 and the scoped rifles.
  2. Every gun uses the same accuracy rule. Each shot adds 0.8 to an accuracy penalty capped at 1.0, which decays at 1.2 per second (2.2 crouched). The first shot uses the gun's tightest cone; from the second shot on, every gun is at or near its widest. The fewer hits a gun needs, the more of those hits land while it is still accurate.
  3. The MX has no climb. It has no camera recoil entry, only a view punch that resets before each shot. Its aimed first shot has zero spread, the same as the ZR68 family and the M41. The default MPN45 climbs by 0.25 to 0.5 degrees per shot.
  4. Support gets the MX at Corporal, the second rank. Assault waits until Sergeant. Support has 225 HP and pays nothing extra for the gun. The MX also has the highest penetration of any automatic primary (38).
  5. The MX scores 55 on the usefulness quotient, fourth of 20 and the best rifle at close and mid range, level with the PZ252 at 39 m. Above it are the SRS (100), AA13 (76) and Supa 7 (60). The SRS and AA13 are Lieutenant-only Assault guns, and both shotguns fall well behind the MX at 39 m. The next rifle is the PZ252 at 48. The UQ is synthetic and leaves out several things; see what it misses.
  6. Controlling the viewkick barely changes any rifle's score; controlling camera recoil does. At today's values the kick is small next to the bloom, so a near-perfect player gains 2 UQ points or less on the standard-kick guns, and nothing on the MX. The camera-recoil guns gain the most: the KYLA from 13 to 34 and the MPN45 from 16 to 31. The kick still costs up to 15% (the Jitte; the MX 7%), but in its random part, which no player can cancel, and at 4 times today's size it would cut most rifles' scores by 30 to 50%. Viewkick and control.

Headshots do chest damage. A head or neck hit does exactly what an upper chest hit does: the gun's damage times 1.485. sk_player_head and sk_player_chest are both 1 in skill.cfg, and a running server reported the same head value on 18 October 2026. Every figure on this page holds for head hits too.

Just in case you're skeptical

These are all the steps a bullet's damage takes from the weapon script to the player's health. Only step 3 looks at where the bullet hit, and it treats head and chest the same.

  1. Script damage enters the bullet. CHL2MP_Player::FireBullets copies the weapon script's Damage onto the bullet (hl2mp_player.cpp:710). A Supa 7 slug gets 2.5 times that here (:716). The NEO weapon base leaves the bullet's damage at zero, so this is the step that sets it.
  2. The global modifier. CNEO_Player::ModifyFireBulletsDamage multiplies by sv_neo_wep_dmg_modifier, 1.485 by default (neo_player.cpp:4058).
  3. The hit group, and the only place it counts. CBasePlayer::TraceAttack scales by sk_player_head, _chest, _stomach, _arm or _leg (player.cpp:1004). No NEO, HL2 or HL2MP player class overrides this function.
  4. Nothing after that reads the hit group. Skill-level scaling does nothing under the multiplayer rules NEO inherits (gamerules.h:312). Players carry no armor outside a spectator takeover (neo_player.cpp:4208). The received damage scale only zeroes damage during TDM and DM spawn protection (neo_player.cpp:2667). OnTakeDamage_Alive only splits damage into whole points and a carried fraction (neo_player.cpp:3321).
  5. The headshot flag changes no damage. It is set on the death event for head and neck hits, and only read for the kill notice (neo_gamerules.cpp:4579).

To check a server yourself, run sk_player_head and sk_player_chest in its console. Both should print 1.

3Breakdown

3.1One accuracy rule for every gun

Spread is a cone that moves between two sizes, one pair for hip fire and one for aiming. The accuracy penalty picks the point between them: 0 is the tight end, 1 is the wide end (GetBulletSpread). No gun overrides the penalty step or the cap, so they differ only in the two cone sizes, the fire rate and the punch.

Firing as fast as the gun allows, the second shot lands at a penalty of about 0.67 and every later shot near 0.87 to 0.93. The pattern below repeats for every automatic: one perfect shot, then the wide cone for the rest of the burst. Crouching lowers the steady state to about 0.77 on the MX.

3.2Firing patterns

Each card is a target at 768 units (about 19.5 m) showing the real hitboxes of the NSF Assault model in its reference pose, an A-pose with the arms about 45 degrees below horizontal. Each hitbox is a box that turns with its bone, drawn here as its true outline from the front. The darker boxes are the ×1.0 tier: head, neck and upper chest. The crosshair is the aim point, the middle of the chest box. Dots are impacts over 50 bursts, aimed, standing, with no aim error and no pull down. The first shot of each burst is pink; the rest are blue. Guns with camera recoil (MPN45, PZ252 from the hip, KYLA, the scoped rifles) drift upward because nobody is pulling the view back down here.

3.3Kill math

Damage has no range falloff. Each bullet's script damage is multiplied by 1.485, then by the hit group: 1.0 for head and chest, 0.75 for stomach and arms, 0.6 for legs (skill.cfg). Class health is 100 for Recon, 120 for Assault and 225 for Support. The table counts hits in the ×1.0 tier.

The player models have the usual seven hit groups, but the multipliers fold them into three tiers. On the NSF Assault model, the high tier (×1.0) is the head, neck and upper chest, from 51 units up; the mid tier (×0.75) is the stomach, pelvis and arms; the low tier (×0.6) is the legs. Only head and neck hits set the headshot flag on the death event, which is used for the kill notice and not for damage.

The MX sits on a breakpoint. Drag the slider to see what one or two points of script damage do.

3.4Usefulness quotient

The UQ is a kill rate. For each gun, class and range, the sim fires 600 engagements with human aim error and finds the fire cadence that kills fastest. Each engagement scores chance of a kill in one magazine divided by (median time to kill + 0.25 s reaction). Those are averaged over the three classes and weighted by range: 35% at 6.5 m (half of it from the hip), 40% at 19.5 m, 25% at 39 m. The result is multiplied by the square root of the gun's move speed relative to the light guns, then scaled so the best primary scores 100.

Forgiveness is the share of that kill rate a gun keeps when the player's aim error doubles. Low forgiveness means the gun punishes a bad day.

The UQ is synthetic. It is a number made up for this page to compare guns on one scale, not a measure of how a gun does in real matches. It comes only from the sim, and the sim is missing several things that decide fights:

  • Moving targets, strafing, jumping and the shooter's own movement.
  • Cover, penetration through walls and partial exposure.
  • Reload, aim-in and weapon switch time, and running out of ammo over a round.
  • Gun-holding poses, which put the arms in front of the chest; the sim uses the model's reference pose.
  • How fights really split across ranges on NEO maps; the 35/40/25 weighting is a guess.
  • Team play, class roles and what each rank can buy.
  • Real players' aim and recoil control; the aim error, pull-down and control profiles (3.7) are assumptions.
  • The Supa 7 slug, the Tachi burst mode and the BALC.

Use it to ask why two guns differ, not to rank guns for balance changes on its own.

3.5Why the MX feels so strong

No single MX value is extreme. It wins because it sits at or near the best value in every column at once:

  • Breakpoint. 3 high-tier hits on Recon and Assault, 6 on Support. The ZR68S, at the same fire rate, needs 4, 4 and 7. Against the real hitboxes, counting misses and stomach or arm hits, the MX has the fastest median kill of the four main rifles (MX, ZR68S, PZ252, ZR68C) in 10 of 11 class and range cases. In the other, an Assault at 19.5 m, it ties the ZR68S at 6 trigger pulls. Aimed at 6.5 m it kills Recon and Assault in the minimum 3.
  • Accuracy where it counts. Zero first-shot cone when aimed, and it needs only 3 hits, so a third of its kill shots come at the tight end of the cone.
  • No climb. Only a 0.25 to 0.5 degree punch that resets every shot, so a held trigger stays on target.
  • Penetration 38. More than any other automatic primary (ZR68C and PZ252 have 28), so it keeps damage through thin cover.
  • Cheap for Support. Corporal on the 225 HP class, ahead of Sergeant on Assault.

What it gives up is small: move speed scale 0.725 against 0.8 for the ZR68C, and a 30 round magazine.

3.7Viewkick and control

Three separate things move a gun's aim after a shot, all in AddViewKick and the shot manipulator. Only the first two are switched on today. Bloom (3.1) picks a random point inside the cone on every shot, and the player can only change the cone's size. Viewkick and camera recoil are different: part of each is predictable, so a player who knows the gun can work against it.

Five control profiles. To see what knowing the kick is worth, the sim adds a player's hand to the same model. Each profile is a set of skills that improve together: aiming against the kick they expect (and how reliable that guess is), correcting what is left once they see it, pulling camera climb back down, and choosing a slower rhythm. Aim error is the same for every profile, and every profile gets the same random numbers, so the differences come from control alone. The profile values are assumptions for comparing guns, not measurements of players.

What it shows.

  • Controlling the kick barely matters at today's values. The rifles, SMGs and shotguns with the standard kick move by 2 points or less from None to Near-perfect, and the MX by none. At the MX's rhythm the kick drops each follow-up shot about 6 units at 19.5 m, while bloom spreads that shot over a cone about 14 units wide. Cancelling the kick moves the track to the middle of the cone without making the cone smaller.
  • The kick still costs most guns something, in the part nobody can control. Removing it entirely (Fig 10) would raise the MX from 55 to 59 (+7%), the PZ252 by 7%, the Tachi by 8%, the MilSO by 10% and the Jitte by 15%. A skilled player only cancels the predictable part, the average up-down pull. What is left is each kick's random size and its random sideways jump of up to 0.6 degrees, about as large as the pull itself. That is why the Near-perfect profile wins back almost nothing even though the kick costs the MX 7%.
  • A downward kick is the forgiving direction. The MX's kick pulls shots from the chest toward the stomach, which is still body. The Jitte's upward kick pulls them toward the neck and over the shoulders, where the body is narrow, so the Jitte and Jitte-S gain 8 to 11% from control and the MX gains nothing.
  • Correcting after seeing the punch needs a slow rhythm. It only works when the gap between shots is longer than the player's reaction, so the gains go to the pistols: the MilSO gains 14% and the Tachi 5%.
  • Camera recoil is where control pays. The KYLA goes from 13 to 34, the MPN45 from 16 to 31, the ZR68L from 23 to 30 and the M41L from 32 to 40. Today's scores already assume Skilled pull-down for these.
  • A bigger kick is a strong lever. At 4 times today's kick, the standard-kick rifles and SMGs lose 30 to 50% of their score: the MX falls from 55 to 32, the ZR68C from 43 to 22. The mechanism is not weak; today's values are small next to the bloom. The shotguns barely change, because their next shot comes after the kick has died away.
  • So viewkick is a lever designers have not used yet. Today the kick is small and mostly random, the opposite of what rewards skill. To make control matter on the main rifles, make the predictable part large and the random part small: a bigger up-down kick with min close to max, a narrow sideways range, and a smaller cone. sv_neo_dynamic_viewkick on its own would only shrink the kick on the first shot, where it has no effect anyway.
  • What the sim cannot see. The kick shakes the screen on every shot, which players feel even when it barely changes where bullets land; that is probably most of what viewkick means to them. And a real player may chase the jumping crosshair and make things worse, which would score below the None profile, since None assumes the player ignores it.

4Supporting detail

How the sim works

  • Per shot, in code order: cone from the current penalty, spread sample (CShotManipulator::ApplySpread, bias 1), fire, penalty + 0.8, punch reset and kick, camera recoil (AddViewKick).
  • View punch is integrated tick by tick at 66 Hz with Source's spring (damping 9, spring 65). The bullet uses the eye angle plus the current punch.
  • Target: the 19 hitboxes of each class's NSF model (Recon nsf_gsf, Assault nsf_gam, Support nsf_ghm), aimed at the middle of the chest box. Each hitbox is a box in its bone's space, turned by the bone; the sim tests the bullet's line against each box and keeps the nearest hit, as ClipRayToHitbox and TraceToStudio do. There are no capsules in this engine branch. Hit multipliers and HP as in 3.3.
  • Human aim: a per-engagement offset and a per-shot wobble, each 0.25 degrees standard deviation. Camera climb is pulled back with a 0.15 s time constant. The spray cards in Fig 2 turn both off.
  • Cadence search: full rate plus 0.15, 0.2, 0.3, 0.45 and 0.67 s between shots. The best one is kept per class and range.
  • Control profiles (3.7): the same model with a hand added. The player aims against the expected punch at their rhythm, guessing its size with a per-engagement error; corrects a share of the remaining punch when the gap between shots is longer than their reaction; pulls camera climb down with the profile's time constant; and only uses rhythms up to the profile's slowest. Each gun, scenario and cadence draws from its own seed, the same for every profile. python3 docs/weapons/sim/control_sim.py, about 3 minutes.
  • Seeded (20261018), pure Python, about 4 minutes: python3 docs/weapons/sim/weapon_sim.py.

What it leaves out

  • Moving targets, strafing and partial cover. These favor automatics over one-shot guns, so the SRS score is an upper bound.
  • Animation pose. The hitboxes are from the reference pose (an A-pose); holding a gun moves the arms in front of the chest, which turns some ×1.0 hits into ×0.75 hits.
  • Reload time, aim-in time (0.115 s for every gun) and switch time.
  • Movement penalty and jumping. A moving shooter from the hip gains 2.0 penalty per second.
  • The Supa 7 slug (2.5 times pellet damage), the Tachi burst mode and the BALC (Juggernaut only).
